Slide 17 · Groups · 10 min

The Anomaly: Connecting Findings to Actions

Choose the most appropriate first step based only on the evidence provided.

Analytic finding: Over the last three Tuesdays, there has been a 15% spike in delayed trains on the Green Line between 7:00 AM and 8:30 AM.
Defend your choice. What does the evidence support, and what still needs investigation?
Facilitator Guide · The Anomaly
Do not reveal the explanation early. Separate what the data shows from what participants think caused it.
Facilitator key: An unusual value is evidence to investigate. The chart alone does not establish cause. Missing records, system outages, disruptions, or real behavior changes can create similar patterns.
Debrief: What do we know? What are we assuming? What source would you check next?

Sort the Scenarios

Classify each scenario as Descriptive, Diagnostic, Predictive, or Prescriptive.

Use these questions: Descriptive = What happened? Diagnostic = Why did it happen? Predictive = What is likely to happen? Prescriptive = What should we do?
Facilitator Guide · Sort the Scenarios
VILT: Send teams to breakout rooms. Direct teams to complete the sort in their Participant Workbook. Keep PowerPoint visible for timing and whole-group instructions.
Facilitator key: Descriptive = what happened. Diagnostic = why it happened. Predictive = what is likely to happen. Prescriptive = what action should be taken.
Debrief: Call on different groups to explain where they placed selected scenarios. Correct the reasoning, not only the category.

Facilitator Guide · Slide 25 Demo
Run the demo in this order.
  1. Open the structured Excel file. Point to one row as one record and one column as one field.
  2. Use the table filter or sort control to show why structure matters.
  3. Place the free-text log beside the structured file. Ask what would happen if you tried to sort the sentences.
  4. Read the first sentence and identify the five values: Date, Railcar, Issue, Location, and Status. Point to the matching first row.
  5. Repeat with the second sentence more quickly, then show the remaining completed rows.
Key point: Someone must decide what the fields are and apply them consistently. Structure makes sorting, filtering, and analysis possible.
Watch for: Participants may assume the conversion happens automatically. Reinforce that inconsistent field choices create messy data before analysis begins.

Is This the Right Data?

Decide which questions the dataset can answer accurately.

Dataset: Monthly summary of track maintenance work orders closed by division.
Why can the dataset answer some questions but not others?
Facilitator Guide · Is This the Right Data?
Focus: Make participants judge fitness for purpose before analysis.
Facilitator key: Listen for source relevance, completeness, time period, granularity, definitions, freshness, and whether the dataset directly supports the question.
Debrief: What decision are we trying to make? What additional data would change your confidence?

Facilitator Guide · Match the Chart
Focus: The question comes before the chart type.
Facilitator key: Bar = compare categories. Line = change over time. Scatter = relationship between two numeric variables.
Debrief: What would make another chart type a poor choice here?

Slide 44 · Groups · 10 min

Finding the Headline

Pass the 10-second test: distill a dashboard into one clear sentence.

The 10-second test: “If you had only 10 seconds to present this dashboard to the General Manager, what is the single sentence you would say?”

Too vague

“Ridership is changing.”

Effective

“Ridership on weekends has surpassed pre-pandemic levels.”

Facilitator Guide · Finding the Headline
Move from topic labels to takeaway headlines.
Facilitator key: A strong headline communicates the finding. Example: 'Red Line delays rose for three consecutive months' is stronger than 'Monthly Delay Data.'
Debrief: If someone read only the headline, would they understand the finding?

Trust, but Verify

Compare the AI interpretation against the source data before using it.

Context is your job. AI may identify a spike in the data without knowing what happened operationally that week.
Facilitator Guide · Trust, but Verify
Human context is required. AI may identify patterns but does not know operational context unless supplied.
Facilitator key: Separate source-supported statements from plausible explanations. A delay spike does not prove a cause.
Debrief: What did AI get right? What did it infer? What would you verify before sharing?

Tailoring the Message

Rewrite a technical finding for a non-technical audience without changing its meaning.

AI PracticeUse Copilot to rewrite, then verify every number and meaning.
Original technical finding: “A 14% degradation in MDBF for the 7000-series fleet was observed in Q2, highly correlated with HVAC subsystem anomalies, prompting a review of preventive maintenance intervals.”
Rewrite this technical finding as a brief, clear executive summary for a non-technical audience. Preserve every number and do not add a cause, recommendation, or certainty that is not in the original.
AIOpen Copilot
Facilitator Guide · Tailoring the Message
Coach for audience and decision.
Facilitator key: Leaders need the finding, operational meaning, and requested action. Remove technical detail that does not help them act while preserving supporting evidence.
Debrief: What does this audience need to know, and what do they need to do?

Slide 48 · Capstone · Pairs · 10 min

Pitching Your Finding

Combine the finding, supporting evidence, and recommended action into a decision-ready pitch.

Focus on: clarity of the message, relevance of the data, and actionability of the recommendation.
Facilitator Guide · Capstone Pitch
Pairs: One Presenter and one Decision-Maker. Allow two minutes for the pitch, feedback, then swap roles.
Facilitator key: Listen for a clear finding, relevant evidence or visual, and a specific recommendation or decision request.
Debrief: Decision-Maker asks: What do you want me to do with this information?

Slide 49 · Peer Review · 10 min

Constructive Review

Give your partner specific feedback on clarity, visuals, and actionability.

Clarity

Was the main finding obvious immediately?

Visuals

Did the chart support the message or distract from it?

Actionability

Did the presenter ask for a specific, data-backed decision?

Facilitator Guide · Constructive Review
Use the rubric to keep feedback specific.
Facilitator key: Clarity = main finding is obvious. Visuals = chart supports rather than distracts. Actionability = presenter asks for a specific, data-backed operational decision or action.
Debrief: Invite volunteers to share the strongest pitch they heard and what made it effective.

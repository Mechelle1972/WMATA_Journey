Using the Participant eWorkbook

Get participants into the workspace and confident about saving their work before the formal start.

5 min before 9:00
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. Post the participant eWorkbook link in chat.
  2. Ask participants to open it in a separate tab and keep it open all day.
  3. Point out section navigation, workspace fields, Download my work, Restore saved work, and Download readable copy.
  4. Resolve access problems before 9:00 a.m.

Watch for

  • A participant who cannot open the workbook.
  • Participants closing the workbook after orientation.

Debrief / listen for

No separate debrief. Keep this step moving.

Transition

At 9:00 a.m., start the title slide.

Full group poll

Where Projects Get Into Trouble

Surface where participants most often see project trouble and frame the course as one connected system.

Opening
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. Run the poll using the platform poll, reactions, or chat.
  2. Ask participants to select the point where their projects most often get into trouble.
  3. Acknowledge the spread of responses without solving them yet.

Watch for

  • Turning the poll into a long problem-solving discussion.

Debrief / listen for

  • Unclear expectations
  • Weak planning
  • Dependencies and resources
  • Risk
  • Ownership
  • Communication and execution

Transition

Introduce Project Atlas as the case the class will use all day.

Learner View Preview
Where Projects Get Into TroubleFull group poll · Opening
  1. Select the point where your projects most often get into trouble.

Expected output: A quick class-level view of recurring trouble spots.

Breakout rooms of four

Make Atlas Planning-Ready

Turn the Atlas brief into planning-ready information.

20 min
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. State the project goal.
  2. Identify the major deliverables.
  3. Define how success will be recognized.
  4. Identify the known constraints.
  5. Identify the assumptions that need validation.

Watch for

  • Task lists instead of a goal.
  • Deliverables described as activities.
  • Assumptions treated as facts.

Debrief / listen for

  • Which line changed the most?
  • Which assumption would you validate first, and who would you ask?

Transition

Move from definition into the plan structure.

Learner View Preview
Make Atlas Planning-ReadyBreakout rooms of four · 20 min
  1. Complete Workspace section 1.
  2. Bring back your goal statement.

Expected output: Bring back one goal statement.

Breakout groups

Build the Atlas Plan

Build an executable part of the Atlas plan that another person could follow.

15 min
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. Choose or assign a major Atlas deliverable.
  2. Enter major work, milestone or completion point, expected timing, one clear owner, and resources or support required.
  3. Choose one entry to test in the debrief.

Watch for

  • Entries with no owner.
  • Detailed schedules instead of planning structure.
  • Activities with no completion point.

Debrief / listen for

  • What made the entry usable to someone outside your group?

Transition

Now test the logic and dependencies between the work.

Learner View Preview
Build the Atlas PlanBreakout groups · 15 min
  1. Build one usable part of the Atlas plan.
  2. Each entry needs major work, milestone, timing, owner, and resources or support.

Expected output: One complete plan entry another person can understand.

Breakout rooms of four

Map the Atlas Dependencies

Sequence Atlas work and identify the points where the plan can bind.

20 min
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. Place the work in the order it must run.
  2. Mark what can run in parallel.
  3. Mark every handoff between teams.
  4. Flag where two items need the same person in the same week.

Watch for

  • Treating every task as sequential.
  • Missing cross-team handoffs.
  • Ignoring shared-resource conflicts.

Debrief / listen for

  • Which dependency matters most and why?

Transition

Use the exposed dependencies to focus the risk conversation.

Learner View Preview
Map the Atlas DependenciesBreakout rooms of four · 20 min
  1. Complete Workspace section 3.
  2. Tag each item Parallel, Handoff, or Conflict.

Expected output: Bring back the tightest handoff.

Breakout rooms of four

Risk the Atlas Plan

Create a focused risk log that changes the Atlas plan.

18 min
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. Log only the top three risks.
  2. For each, complete impact, likelihood, priority, mitigation, owner, and contingency or trigger.
  3. Name the plan change or decision the risk creates.

Watch for

  • Long lists of worries.
  • Risks with no owner.
  • Risks that do not change timing, ownership, communication, or a decision.

Debrief / listen for

  • What changed in your plan because of the risk?

Transition

Move from risk ownership into stakeholder commitment.

Learner View Preview
Risk the Atlas PlanBreakout rooms of four · 18 min
  1. Complete Workspace section 4.
  2. If a risk changes nothing in the plan, challenge whether it belongs in the top three.

Expected output: Bring back one risk that changed the plan.

Pairs

The Commitment Slipped

Practice opening an accountability conversation when a commitment slips.

12 min
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. Partner A opens the conversation.
  2. State what was expected.
  3. Confirm what happened.
  4. Name the project impact.
  5. Agree on the next commitment and clarify follow-through.
  6. Partner B responds, then says which part of the opening landed.

Watch for

  • Rambling openings.
  • Softening the impact until the need is unclear.
  • Blame language.

Debrief / listen for

  • Which part of the opening made the next commitment easier to reach?

Transition

Use that thinking to complete the commitment plan.

Learner View Preview
The Commitment SlippedPairs · 12 min
  1. Practice the opening only. You do not need to resolve the full conversation.
  2. Keep the focus on the commitment and project impact.

Expected output: A clear next commitment without blame language.

Individual, then main room

Commitment and Accountability Plan

Complete the commitment plan for Atlas.

10 min
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. Select a critical stakeholder.
  2. Name the commitment needed.
  3. Name the owner of the relationship and planned touchpoint.
  4. Define the accountability mechanism.
  5. Identify a signal of slippage and the planned response.
  6. Answer which commitment will be hardest to get and what would make it easier.

Watch for

  • Communication tasks that do not secure a commitment or decision.
  • Vague stakeholder labels.

Debrief / listen for

  • Which commitment will be hardest to get, and what would make it easier?

Transition

Shift from alignment into the execution rhythm.

Learner View Preview
Commitment and Accountability PlanIndividual, then main room · 10 min
  1. Complete Workspace section 5.
  2. Bring back your hardest commitment.

Expected output: One specific commitment with a signal of slippage and response.

Breakout rooms of four

Atlas Control Room

Run an execution check-in, decide what changes, and write the update.

16 min
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. Read the scenario: a milestone is behind and a dependency is incomplete. Launch has not moved.
  2. Decide whether the plan changes or the slip is absorbed.
  3. Name what must be escalated and to whom.
  4. Name the stakeholder who needs to hear it first.
  5. Draft the agenda items and status message.

Watch for

  • Avoiding the decision by saying to monitor it.
  • Status updates that describe the problem but not the decision or next action.

Debrief / listen for

  • What did you decide?
  • Read one sentence from the status message.

Transition

Send participants to the second 10-minute break.

Learner View Preview
Atlas Control RoomBreakout rooms of four · 16 min
  1. Complete Workspace section 6.
  2. Make the call, then write the update.

Expected output: Bring back the status message and the absorb-or-change decision.

Individual

Copilot Lab: Project Plan and Meeting Agenda

Generate two draft artifacts and compare them to the project facts participants already built.

15 min
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. Round 1: Generate a planning structure from the Atlas goal, deliverables, milestones, owners, and timing.
  2. Compare it with Workspace sections 1 and 2.
  3. Round 2: Generate a short agenda for the next Atlas check-in.
  4. Compare it with the execution rhythm and meeting guidance.
  5. Mark what you would keep, change, or reject.

Watch for

  • Treating polished wording as evidence of accuracy.
  • Missing owners or milestones.
  • Agenda items that do not lead to decisions.

Debrief / listen for

  • What did the tool structure well?
  • What was missing or unsupported?

Transition

Move to risk log and status report outputs.

Learner View Preview
Copilot Lab: Project Plan and Meeting AgendaIndividual · 15 min
  1. Use Project Atlas only. Do not use sensitive or personal data.
  2. Create both artifacts, then check them against your own work.

Expected output: Two drafts plus a human review of what to keep, change, or reject.

Individual

Copilot Lab: Risk Log and Status Report

Generate a risk log and status report, then verify both against the Atlas facts.

15 min
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. Round 3: Improve the Atlas risk log while keeping your top three risks.
  2. Check what the tool added and whether you agree.
  3. Round 4: Draft a status report for the execution scenario.
  4. Identify at least one useful contribution and one weak or unsupported assumption.

Watch for

  • Unsupported assumptions.
  • Risk owners missing.
  • Status reports that bury the decision needed.

Debrief / listen for

  • What did you keep?
  • What did you reject, and why?

Transition

Have participants choose one output to verify and refine.

Learner View Preview
Copilot Lab: Risk Log and Status ReportIndividual · 15 min
  1. Check every addition against the Atlas facts.
  2. If a statement is unsupported, remove or rewrite it.

Expected output: Two reviewed drafts with unsupported content identified.

Breakout rooms of four

Capstone: Re-plan Under Pressure

Update Atlas under pressure and name what the team will protect.

20 min
Open Participant eWorkbook

Instructor flow

  1. Update the plan.
  2. Update risk.
  3. Identify who needs a new commitment or decision.
  4. Draft what the sponsor needs to hear.
  5. Name one tradeoff.
  6. Name one thing you will not give up and why.

Watch for

  • Compressing dates without changing scope, owner, sequence, or risk.
  • Trying to protect everything.

Debrief / listen for

  • What changed?
  • What did you protect, and why?

Transition

Bring the lesson back to participants' real work.

Learner View Preview
Capstone: Re-plan Under PressureBreakout rooms of four · 20 min
  1. Complete Workspace section 8.
  2. You cannot protect everything. Name what changes and what you will not give up.

Expected output: Bring back one decision, the reasoning, and one protected item.

Facilitator cue: Use one or two sample entries to test whether someone outside the group could follow the plan.

Major work + milestone

What the work is and how completion is recognized.

Owner + timing

Who owns it and when it needs to happen.

Resources / support

What the owner needs to succeed.

ELECTRONIC WHITEBOARD

Dependency Map Debrief

Facilitator cue: Capture the binding points, not the entire sequence.

Parallel work

What can move at the same time.

Handoffs

Where one team waits on another.

Conflicts

Shared people, timing, or resource pressure.

ELECTRONIC WHITEBOARD

Risk Log Debrief

Facilitator cue: Only capture risks that changed the plan or a decision.

Risk + impact

What could happen and why it matters.

Mitigation + owner

What reduces the risk and who owns the action.

Trigger / contingency

What signal causes the response to start.

ELECTRONIC WHITEBOARD

Accountability Conversation Debrief

Facilitator cue: Keep examples specific and neutral. Do not turn the board into a list of blame statements.

Expected / happened

Neutral statement of the agreement and gap.

Project impact

How the missed commitment affects Atlas.

Next commitment / follow-through

What happens next and how it will be tracked.

ELECTRONIC WHITEBOARD

Commitment Plan Debrief

Facilitator cue: Capture one representative example from a few participants.

Critical stakeholder

Who matters most for this commitment.

Commitment needed

Specific action, decision, or support.

Signal + response

How slippage is detected and what happens next.

ELECTRONIC WHITEBOARD

Atlas Control Room Debrief

Facilitator cue: Push groups to make the decision. “Monitor it” is not the decision.

Absorb or change

The decision and the project reason.

Escalation / audience

What must move upward and who hears first.

Status message

ELECTRONIC WHITEBOARD

ELECTRONIC WHITEBOARD

Capstone Report-Out

Facilitator cue: Take one decision and one protected item from each group. Avoid solving the capstone for them.

What the group will not give up and what they are willing to trade.
